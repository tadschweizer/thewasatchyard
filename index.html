Race format

Same loop. Every hour.

Every hour, everyone starts together. You cover 4.167 miles, get whatever rest you earn, and come right back to the line to do it again. Twenty-four laps add up to 100 miles, and the race only ends when a single runner finishes a lap alone.

What each lap asks of you

  • Controlled pacing on mixed trail from the first lap on
  • Patience once the field starts getting tired
  • Quick decisions in the crew area between laps

This race gets decided by the runners who stay steady the longest.

Common questions

Backyard ultra questions, answered

The questions we get most often about the format, the course, and race day.

What is a backyard ultra?

A backyard ultra is a last-runner-standing race. Everyone starts the same 4.167-mile loop together at the top of every hour. Finish inside the hour and you get whatever time is left as rest. Miss the start or miss the cutoff and your race is over. The winner is the only runner who completes a loop that nobody else finishes.

We wrote a longer explanation in our backyard ultra guide.

Why is the loop 4.167 miles?

4.167 miles per hour works out to exactly 100 miles in 24 hours. It is the standard backyard ultra distance, so lap counts from The Wasatch Yard compare directly to any other backyard race.

Where is The Wasatch Yard held?

Round Valley in Park City, Utah. The start and finish are on Gillmor Way, and the loop runs through gravel, greenway, and single-track. Full course details are on the race information page.

Is this a good first backyard ultra?

Yes. The loop is honest but not brutal, the crew area is right at the start line, and an evening start means you learn what night running feels like early. Bring a headlamp, layers, and a fueling plan you have already tested.

How long does the race last?

There is no scheduled finish. The race runs until one runner completes a loop alone. Plan for a long night, and tell your crew to plan for longer than you think.

Can I have a crew?

Yes. You get access to your crew and your gear between laps in a dedicated crew area next to the start line. There is no time to waste in there, so set your space up before the first whistle.
